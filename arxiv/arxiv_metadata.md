**Abstract (plain text, for the form):**
Radar micro-Doppler classifiers of daily activities are usually evaluated with splits that share people and rooms between training and test. We evaluate a 5.8 GHz FMCW activity classifier under leave-one-campaign-out (LOCO) testing on a public seven-campaign dataset that includes two care sites with older adults. Macro-F1 drops from 0.948 with random splits to 0.912 under LOCO and to 0.860 on the care sites, where calibration error doubles. We then test low-cost remedies with paired statistics over 5-10 seeds: target-tracking range gates, CFAR-style noise-floor referencing, a dual-reference input, augmentation, ImageNet pre-training, label-free test-time adaptation (AdaBN, TENT) and enrollment of up to three labelled residents. The best combinations gain 1-2 points overall, but none narrows the laboratory-to-care-site gap of about seven points. The residual errors (pick-up vs. drinking) are consistent with cohort-level kinematic differences rather than site or sensor effects.
